About

I received my PhD in Finance from Imperial College London in 2026. I am a Visiting Researcher at the Imperial Centre of Excellence in Quantitative Finance (opens in a new tab). Alongside my research, I work as an independent quantitative research and AI/ML consultant to investment teams (independent advisory).
I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

My primary research fields are empirical asset pricing, machine learning and big data in finance, and financial econometrics. Working with large panels of stock characteristics and regularised machine-learning estimators, I ask which parts of a factor model’s answer (its factors, its alphas, its portfolios) reflect information in the data, and which reflect only how the model measures that information. I follow these measurement choices from identification and inference to the portfolios an investor ends up holding.

Research

Job market paper

The Ruler Matters: Factor Exposures, Attribution, and Portfolio Risk

Working paper, October 2026 · 133 pages including the Internet Appendix

Seminar and conference presentations: University of Cambridge, Faculty of Economics, 4 November 2026 (invited; scheduled); EPFL, Lausanne, 16 June 2026 (opens in a new tab) (invited seminar; precursor presented as No-Arbitrage Factor Geometry)

  • Paper page
  • Paper (PDF)

Firm characteristics are widely used to estimate conditional factor loadings, and a larger library can fit returns better. This paper asks which stock positions an estimated loading space represents, and what that implies for return attribution and portfolio risk. The cross-sectional second moment of characteristics serves as a ruler (a metric, or inner product, on characteristic space, given by the characteristics’ Gram matrix): loading directions are compared by the stocks they select, not by their coefficients.

In U.S. equity data, with the factor count fixed, expanding the characteristic library of an instrumented principal component analysis (IPCA) model from 36 to 153 characteristics raises in-sample fit, yet the estimated loading space represents momentum and size positions less fully. Reallocating exposure shared by observed and latent factors changes reported contributions without changing joint fitted returns. With coefficients fixed, portfolios that measure exposure on current rather than annual-origin stocks have lower subsequent variance at the same characteristic target in all twelve comparisons, and nine of the twelve simultaneous 95% intervals exclude zero.

Locators: library expansion (Section I.B, pp. 4–5; Section IV, p. 22); attribution (Section I.B, p. 5); current versus annual-origin exposure (Section VI.B, p. 32; Figure 6, p. 35). The twelve current-versus-origin comparisons are exploratory and compare two complete portfolio policies (p. 6).

Working papers

Same Forecasts, Different Portfolios: Factor Normalization and Regularization

Working paper, October 2026 · 116 pages including the Internet Appendix

Two separately estimated IPCA specifications that differ only in how the loadings are normalised (Euclidean or Gram metric) give nearly identical forecasts but gross Sharpe ratios of 0.69 and 1.07 under the same numerical penalty on factor covariances. The paper derives the penalty transformation that keeps portfolio positions unchanged across equivalent model representations; applying it aligns the two portfolios (p. 3).

Characteristic-Space Metrics in Factor Models: Identification and Inference

SSRN preprint, September 2026

Two factor models can give every stock the same fitted return yet credit it differently to latent factors, observed factors and an intercept; the paper shows that the metric (inner product) chosen for characteristic space, a measurement convention or “ruler”, decides that split, and derives confidence intervals that carry the metric’s own estimation error.

Interpreting Estimated Pricing Errors: Evidence from Characteristic-Based Return Forecasts

Working paper, September 2026

Pairing each model’s expected-return estimate with each model’s factors on the same U.S. stocks, the paper finds that the alpha gap between equal- and value-weighted training mainly reflects different expected-return estimates, and that alpha magnitude adds little to forecast selection (exploratory evidence).

Research in progress

Exposure or Intercept? Identifying Corporate Bond Premia with No-Arbitrage IPCA

With Paolo Zaffaroni · In preparation

Using No-Arbitrage IPCA (NA-IPCA) on public TRACE data, the paper asks whether corporate bond characteristics predict returns through factor exposures or through an intercept the factors leave unexplained, and how much of that split depends on the choice of ruler (the metric) and on the cap on how much the intercept may carry.
