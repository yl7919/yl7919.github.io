Projects

What I have built and tested for investment research, and what each piece shows.

I build and test expected-return models on large U.S. stock-characteristic panels, and the tools that take a model to a portfolio and make that step checkable. My research (PhD in Finance, Imperial College London, 2026) studies regularised and latent-factor estimators on 36 to 153 characteristics built from CRSP/Compustat; I work in Python (NumPy, pandas, SciPy, scikit-learn) and MATLAB. Outside the papers, I helped build the Imperial Student Investment Fund’s Python research and trading platform. Return-State Behavior Profile

Portfolio behaviour and risk diagnostics

How did a return stream behave in normal months, strong rallies, recoveries, downturns and tail months? From dated strategy and benchmark returns, the function computes conditional returns, downside beta and hit frequencies for each market state, with observation counts and small-sample warnings.

  • Example data: an archived systematic long–short U.S. equity strategy backtest, monthly data Apr 2007 – Nov 2025 (224 months); a research case study, not an audited track record, and a configuration selected from several in an earlier study (selection bias not corrected); its signal loadings were estimated on a sample that may overlap the backtest period, so it is not an out-of-sample result.
  • Interactive demonstration: 7 return streams (four archived, three built from public market and Treasury-bill returns) × 3 state definitions on the full sample, 21 results computed at build time. Half-sample results are not published, because they would let single months of the private stream be reconstructed.
  • Not claimed: future returns, actual trading, or a factor swap on fixed data; a new factor needs a new backtest before these diagnostics apply.

Macro Forecast Evaluation & Regime Diagnostics

Forecast evaluation workbench

How should competing forecasts be judged when economic relationships change? From per-origin forecast records, the function scores models on the same origins, horizon and target, tracks the cumulative loss differential and tests the null of equal predictive accuracy (Diebold–Mariano test), next to a monitor of inflation persistence.

  • Example data: UK services inflation, February 2026 research snapshot; ONS monthly data through Dec 2025.
  • Interactive demonstration: output version, horizon, model and comparison window switch among results computed at build time from the archived records.
  • Not claimed: a forecast, an operational forecasting service, or that one model forecasts better in general.

Terms used on these pages. To say how much of a stock’s expected return each driver explains, a model must first decide when two combinations of characteristics are really different, and how large each one is; the rule it uses is the ruler (formally, a metric: an inner product on combinations of characteristics). A combination (a direction) gives every stock a score; it is not a portfolio. The plain ruler (the Euclidean or identity metric) judges a direction by its weights alone; the characteristic ruler (the Gram metric, taken from the characteristics’ cross-sectional Gram matrix) judges it by the scores it gives real stocks. A ruler is a measurement convention, not a better model. IPCA (instrumented principal component analysis) is a latent-factor model whose loadings are linear in stock characteristics; NA-IPCA (No-Arbitrage IPCA) rebuilds it on the characteristic ruler and adds a capped intercept, the part of expected return no factor in the model explains.

Machine learning and portfolios

Factor-model forecasts, rolling U.S. backtests and controlled comparisons, followed to holdings, risk and costs.

Measured · working paper, October 2026

Which positions a factor model represents: exposures, hedges and holdings

Expanding an IPCA library from 36 to 153 U.S. stock characteristics raises in-sample fit, yet the model’s factors represent momentum and size positions less fully. Measured on the stocks actually held, a hedge built with the plain ruler leaves 46.10% of a value position, by squared stock weight, still lined up with the model’s factor loadings (pp. 4–6). A check to run before trusting a better-fitting model for hedging.

  • 225 monthly formation dates, March 2007 to November 2025; models re-estimated each March on the previous 240 months, 19 times (Section II.A, p. 9; Table I, p. 10).
  • Three latent factors plus six observed ones (market, size, value, profitability, investment and momentum), with and without a characteristic intercept, on 36 and 153 Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen characteristics in 11 economic groups (Section II.B, p. 11; Table I, p. 10).
  • Stocks split permanently into disjoint source and target groups by a fixed hash: the factors are learned on one group and evaluated on the other (p. 9).
  • Hedging rules compared at the same characteristic target: the plain-ruler (coefficient) hedge, the loading-neutral hedge measured on current stocks, and a historical minimum-variance hedge (Section V, pp. 24–25).
  • Gross holdings, one-way turnover and subsequent variance for every policy, with simultaneous intervals over families of twelve comparisons from a twelve-month block bootstrap (Section II.C, pp. 12–15); turnover measures trading volume, not costs (p. 13).
