Abstract

Characteristic-based factor models can fit returns identically while attributing them differently to latent, observed-factor, and intercept components. We show how an inner product on characteristic space selects this decomposition and formulate its loading class modulo latent rotations. For an exactly profiled estimator with deterministic date weights and an estimated Gram metric, we derive a linear representation retaining both return-score and metric uncertainty. Smooth reports admit chart-invariant inference and feasible long-run covariance estimation. Covariance alignment provides sufficient conditions for structural interpretation with a fixed cross section. Near a smooth exposure boundary, metric estimation also moves the feasible set. Joint Gaussian projection yields drift-robust scalar confidence intervals. For one constraint, the least-favorable tail envelopes reduce to the interior and binding endpoints. Numerical experiments distinguish omitted metric uncertainty from omitted boundary movement.

Keywords: characteristic-based factor models; metric-indexed identification; estimated Gram metrics; rotation-invariant inference; inference under inequality constraints. JEL classification: C13, C38, C58, G12.

The paper states the general result of which Proposition 1 of Characteristic Geometry and Portfolio Choice is the portfolio case: a matched change of the metric on characteristic space leaves every fitted return and residual unchanged while the loadings, the factor units and the attribution across factor blocks change.
