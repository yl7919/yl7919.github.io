Research Summary

  • Research programme: my papers study the ruler, the metric a characteristic-based factor model uses to measure and compare directions in characteristic space. The ruler adds no information, yet it can decide which factors a model identifies, how fitted returns are split between factors and alpha, and which portfolio an investor holds. Central paper: The Ruler Matters.
  • Methods: characteristic-based and latent-factor models (IPCA and No-Arbitrage IPCA) on large U.S. stock-characteristic panels; identification and inference; rolling out-of-sample portfolio evaluation. Author of naipca, a Python toolkit for IPCA-family factor estimation.
  • Teaching: Graduate Teaching Assistant in Financial Statistics (student rating 4.8 out of 5; nominated twice for the Excellence in Teaching Award); mentor at the Imperial Student Investment Fund, which works as a student finance lab.
  • Forecast evaluation: built a workflow that compares forecasting models on the same forecast origins, tests whether differences in forecast accuracy are statistically significant (Diebold–Mariano) and shows how far a model ranking depends on the evaluation window and output version (archived UK services-inflation example, 3- and 6-month horizons, pseudo-out-of-sample design with look-ahead documented).

Research Fields

Empirical asset pricing (cross-sectional return prediction, characteristic-based factor models); machine learning and big data in finance (regularised and latent-factor estimators on large characteristic panels); financial econometrics (identification and inference in high-dimensional factor models); portfolio choice and risk management.

Research

Working Papers

Job market paper The Ruler Matters: Factor Exposures, Attribution, and Portfolio Risk

Working paper, October 2026

Seminar and conference presentations: University of Cambridge, Faculty of Economics, 4 November 2026 (invited; scheduled); EPFL, Lausanne, 16 June 2026 (invited seminar; precursor presented as No-Arbitrage Factor Geometry)

Uses the cross-sectional second moment (Gram matrix) of characteristics as a ruler for IPCA loading spaces. Expanding from 36 to 153 characteristics can raise in-sample fit yet leave momentum and size positions less fully represented; reallocating shared exposures changes attribution, not joint fitted returns. With coefficients fixed, exposure references change holdings, trading and subsequent risk.

PDF

Characteristic-Space Metrics in Factor Models: Identification and Inference

SSRN preprint, September 2026. DOI: 10.2139/ssrn.7445120

Factor models with identical fit can credit returns differently to latent factors, observed factors and an intercept; fit cannot choose the split, but the inner product chosen for characteristic space does. The paper derives rotation-invariant inference that carries the Gram metric’s estimation error, and drift-robust confidence intervals near an intercept cap (exposure budget).

PDF · Online supplement · SSRN

A Geometric Framework for Identification in Characteristic-Based Factor Models

SSRN preprint, March 2026 (preliminary version). DOI: 10.2139/ssrn.7013178. Grows out of the PhD thesis; its inference theory is developed further in Characteristic-Space Metrics.

Using a metric taken from the long-run cross-sectional distribution of firm characteristics, the paper derives NA-IPCA, which nests IPCA, caps how much the intercept may carry and admits rotation-invariant inference. In the headline calibrated simulation, NA-IPCA recovers the latent pricing span and an intercept-free IPCA benchmark does not; that design does not isolate the metric.

PDF · SSRN

Same Forecasts, Different Portfolios: Factor Normalization and Regularization

Working paper, October 2026

Two IPCA fits under different normalisations give nearly identical forecasts but different gross Sharpe ratios: a ridge added to factor covariances penalises exposures in units the normalisation sets. Writing the penalty consistently aligns the portfolios. Using the full fitted mean, intercept included, raises the gross Sharpe ratio; its net advantage since October 2007 is imprecise.

PDF

Interpreting Estimated Pricing Errors: Evidence from Characteristic-Based Return Forecasts

Working paper, September 2026

What does a smaller estimated pricing error (alpha) tell a model user? On 153 U.S. characteristics, crossing the expected-return estimates and factor spaces of one model’s equal- and value-weighted fits shows that their gap in fitted intercept magnitudes mainly reflects different estimated means; residual diagnostics add little to forecast selection (exploratory evidence).

Asks, with No-Arbitrage IPCA on public TRACE data, whether bond characteristics predict returns through factor exposure or an intercept, and how much of that split the choice of ruler (the metric) and the intercept cap decide.

PhD Thesis

No-Arbitrage IPCA: A Framework for Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing

PhD thesis, Imperial College London; degree conferred August 2026. Thesis available on request.

Extends IPCA, via the empirical characteristic metric, to nested QZ-IPCA and NA-IPCA models separating latent, observable (Fama–French five factors plus momentum) and residual-pricing components under an intercept cap; gives identification results, an asymptotic linear expansion and projected alternating least squares; simulations and a Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen U.S. panel show near-identical fit coexisting with different identified pricing directions.
